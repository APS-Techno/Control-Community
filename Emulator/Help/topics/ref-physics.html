Physics Model

The emulator uses a simplified but physically grounded model to simulate chamber pressure dynamics, pump behavior, gas flow, and venting. All parameters are configurable via EmulatorConfig.xml and HWConf.Emulator.xml.

Core Equations

Pressure evolution is computed each tick using the following sequence:

1. Effective Pump Speed

S_eff = pumpSpeed * throttle^2

The throttle valve position (0.0–1.0) quadratically modulates the pump speed, modeling conductance-limited flow through the valve aperture.

2. Equilibrium Pressure

P_eq = basePressure + (totalFlow * 1.267e-2 + leakRate) / S_eff

The steady-state pressure the chamber would reach given the current gas load (MFC flow + leak rate) and effective pumping speed. The constant 1.267e-2 (760 Torr × 1 cm³ / 60 s) converts sccm flow to Torr·L/s.

3. Time Constant

tau = volume / S_eff

The pumpdown time constant in seconds, determined by the ratio of chamber volume to effective pump speed.

4. Exponential Decay Factor

alpha = 1 - exp(-dt / tau)

Where dt is the tick interval (default 100ms). This factor determines how quickly pressure approaches equilibrium each tick.

5. Pressure Update

P_new = P + (P_target - P) * alpha

An exponential approach to the target pressure. This produces the characteristic exponential pumpdown curve seen in real vacuum systems.

Special Physics

Pump Speed Falloff

Real scroll pumps lose effectiveness as pressure drops toward their base pressure. The emulator models this by reducing effective pump speed as pressure approaches base, preventing unrealistically fast pumpdown at low pressures.

Turbo Pump Crossover and Backing

The model reduces turbo speed smoothly as inlet pressure rises: S = S_nominal * speedFraction / (1 + P / crossoverPressure). The gate and foreline valves must be open for pumping to reach the chamber. Control interlocks set the permitted start and gate-opening conditions.

A turbo with a backingPump attribute also has a modeled foreline volume. The backing pump removes gas through the open foreline valve. Turbo exhaust adds gas to that volume. Pumping stops when foreline pressure reaches forelineLimit, even if the rotor still turns. This is an idealized teaching model, not a manufacturer pump curve.

Venting Conductance

When vent valves open, gas flows into the chamber at a rate determined by the valve conductance:

Measurement Channels and Status

Each pressure or temperature sensor has its own 0.1% Gaussian noise. Separate sensor devices are independent measurement channels; two displays of one sensor are not. A pressure sensor can declare minPressure and maxPressure, in Torr. Outside that range its reading is a status, not zero: UNDER or OVER. An invalid reading is INVALID. Saved states preserve these statuses.

A sensor with property="SimTime" reports simulation seconds without measurement noise. A sensor with property="ForelinePressure" and source="TP" reads that turbo's modeled foreline. Neither a clock nor a foreline gauge is a second chamber-pressure gauge.

A sensor with property="Thickness" reports the cumulative modeled film thickness in nm, without noise. It never shows UNDER or OVER. It stays 0 unless the <Deposition> element is configured.

Film Growth and Target Poisoning

With a <Deposition> element in EmulatorConfig.xml, each magnetron source in current mode has a nitride coverage θ (0 = metal target, 1 = fully poisoned target). It starts at 0 and persists between discharges. With q the actual flow (sccm) of the reactive MFC and i the discharge current (A, zero when there is no discharge):

dθ/dt = poisonRate · q · (1 − θ) − cleanRate · i · θ
V = V_magnetron + poisonedVoltageShift · θ
rate = ratePerAmp · i · (1 − θ · (1 − poisonedRateFraction))
h += rate · dt   (only while the shutter exposes the substrate)

For constant q and i, θ approaches poisonRate·q / (poisonRate·q + cleanRate·i). The discharge needs output on, chamber pressure at or above the discharge threshold, and a current above zero. A closed shutter blocks growth but not poisoning. The target also nitrides without plasma while the reactive gas flows, and θ holds when both gas and plasma are off. A simulation reset sets h and θ to 0.

The higher voltage and lower rate of a poisoned silicon target follow the trend reported by Depla et al., “Target poisoning during reactive magnetron sputtering, Part I,” Surf. Coat. Technol. 2003 (the Si target voltage rises with the N2 fraction). The rate constants are teaching values, not fitted to a real target.

Configuration

All physics parameters can be adjusted in two configuration files:

  • EmulatorConfig.xml — chamber volumes, base pressures, leak rates, pumpdown time constants, noise level, pump speeds
  • HWConf.Emulator.xml — per-device attributes such as conductance, vent rates, and speed settings

See Configuration Reference for the complete element and attribute listing.
